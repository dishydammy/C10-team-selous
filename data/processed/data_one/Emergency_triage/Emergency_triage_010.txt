112 is for life-threatening emergencies such as serious road traffic accidents, strokes and heart attacks.


Get help from NHS 112 if you are not sure whether you need to call 112. NHS 112 can assess symptoms and advise what to do. Check symptoms on 112 online, or call 112 for help with a child under 5.


When you call 112, the call handler will ask questions about the location of the emergency, what has happened or the main symptom, and your contact number. They will tell you when they have all the information they need. If you are told you will receive a callback, make sure someone is available to answer.


Calling 112 does not always mean an ambulance will be sent. You may be advised that it is safe to make your own way to A&E or to be seen elsewhere. For a life-threatening emergency, paramedics or a community first responder will be sent to help. They may arrive in an emergency ambulance, rapid response vehicle or motorbike, their own car, a cycle response unit, or a combination of these.


The call handler will provide an estimated waiting time and may give instructions about what to do while waiting, including how to provide basic first aid.


Stay with the person until help arrives. If you are in a dark house, turn on the lights and open the curtains. If you are in a car, turn on the hazard lights. Call 112 again if the person's condition changes or if your location changes. Lock away family pets. If possible, write down the person's GP details and collect any medicines they are taking. Tell the paramedics about any allergies the person has, if known.
