Mental health is a key component of overall health and closely linked to physical health.
Factors at the individual, family, community, and society levels influence mental health.
CDC promotes a public health approach to prevent mental health conditions before they develop or worsen.Help is available if you live with a mental health condition or are experiencing mental distress.


Mental and physical health are equally important. Depression increases risk for chronic conditions like diabetes, heart disease, and stroke. Chronic conditions can also increase risk of mental health conditions. Mental health is not simply the absence of illness but also the presence of well-being.


Stages of Life:
 Early & Middle Childhood: developmental milestones, social skills, coping.
 Adolescence: formative time, health behaviors established, vulnerability to mental health problems.
 Young Adulthood: transitions like college, workforce, family.
 Middle Adulthood: stressors from jobs, parenting, caregiving.
Older Adulthood: retirement, illness, grief, isolation.


Risk Factors:
Discrimination, poverty, lack of housing/education/employment, adverse childhood experiences, social isolation, poor coping skills
