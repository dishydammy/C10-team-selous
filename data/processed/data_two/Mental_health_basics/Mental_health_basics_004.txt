Child and adolescent mental health includes how children think, feel, cope, and relate to others as they grow. Emotional wellbeing during childhood affects learning, friendships, behaviour, and later development. Children can experience anxiety, sadness, anger, grief, bullying stress, fear, or behavioural changes, and these issues deserve attention when they are persistent or are affecting daily life.

Common signs of emotional distress in children may include sudden irritability, crying, clinginess, difficulty concentrating, poor sleep, school refusal, changes in appetite, or frequent argument with parents or teachers. Some children may become withdrawn, angry, aggressive, or unusually fearful. Behavioural changes can also be a sign of stress, trauma, bullying, family strain, or a change in routine.

Children who are experiencing bullying, violence at home or school, grief, or significant stress may develop mental health difficulties. Schools are an important place to notice changes because teachers and school staff often see patterns in behaviour, mood, and concentration. A child should be supported by trusted adults and, when appropriate, referred to a health professional or school counsellor.

Parents and caregivers can help by listening calmly, preserving routines, providing reassurance, and noticing whether the child’s symptoms are persistent or worsening. It is also important to involve professionals early if the child has panic symptoms, severe sadness, self-harm thoughts, aggression, or big changes in behaviour. Adult support is crucial, especially when the child cannot cope on their own.

The goal is not to diagnose a child based on one behaviour. It is to identify when the child needs more support and to avoid normalising serious distress. Children do not need to “just get over it,” and changes in mood or functioning should be taken seriously when they persist.
