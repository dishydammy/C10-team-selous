Mental health and emotional wellbeing refer to how a person thinks, feels, relates to others, and copes with daily life. Good mental wellbeing is not simply the absence of distress; it also includes the ability to manage stress, build relationships, cope with difficulties, and feel supported. Mental health can change across life and is affected by relationships, sleep, finances, illness, trauma, and life transitions.

Common signs of emotional distress include feeling overwhelmed, tearful, irritable, unusually tired, isolated, or unable to concentrate. Some people may feel physically tense, restless, or have sleep problems. Others may find it hard to enjoy activities they normally enjoy. These experiences can be linked to stress, relationship issues, work or school pressure, bereavement, trauma, or long-term health problems. They do not necessarily mean a person has a mental illness, but they deserve attention and support.

It is important to recognise that mental health needs can be personal and can affect anyone. People may be reluctant to talk about them because of stigma, privacy concerns, or fear of being judged. A supportive response is to listen without judging, encourage professional help where needed, and help the person access practical support. This may include talking with a trusted family member, school counsellor, community worker, or primary care professional.

For children and adults, the best approach is early support. This can include normalising feelings, teaching coping skills, maintaining routine, sleeping well, eating regularly, getting physical activity, and reducing isolation. People should also be encouraged to seek professional help when symptoms persist, worsen, or interfere with daily functioning, work, study, relationships, or safety.

Mental health support is not about diagnosing a person in a casual conversation. The role of a general health resource is to explain common experiences, signs, and where support can be found. Some people need counselling, therapy, social support, or medical review for conditions that may affect mood or anxiety. A doctor or mental health professional can help assess what is needed.
