General medical danger signs describe symptoms that suggest a person may be seriously unwell and needs urgent assessment. These include altered consciousness, severe breathing problems, severe bleeding, severe dehydration, chest pain, persistent high fever with worsening symptoms, seizures, severe weakness, or shock. The exact danger sign depends on the person’s age, context, and other symptoms, but the broad principle is that red flags should not be managed at home without professional assessment.

Altered consciousness may mean the person is unusually sleepy, difficult to wake, confused, disoriented, or not responding normally. A person may be very weak, unable to stand, or unable to speak clearly. These are warning signs that something serious may be happening. Severe breathlessness, blue lips, chest pain, and severe wheezing are also urgent.

Severe bleeding should be treated urgently. This includes heavy bleeding from a wound, ongoing bleeding after an injury, vomiting blood, passing blood in stool, or severe bleeding during pregnancy. Shock may present as cold, clammy skin, confusion, fast heartbeat, faintness, or collapse. These features require emergency treatment.

Severe dehydration may be seen in vomiting, diarrhoea, or fever, particularly in infants and children. Severe weakness, fainting, little or no urine, confusion, and inability to keep fluids down are warning signs. A person with these features should seek help immediately. For pregnant people, severe bleeding, severe headache, visual changes, or severe abdominal pain may signal complications that need urgent attention.

People with a new seizure, seizure in a child with fever, persistent severe headache, neck stiffness, or a sudden change in sensation or strength should also be assessed urgently. Any symptom that suggests the body is failing to maintain circulation, oxygenation, or consciousness needs immediate attention. This includes unexpected collapse, trouble breathing, or severe pain with weakness.

At home, the main role of caregivers is to recognise danger signs, keep the person safe, and arrange urgent assistance. They should avoid delaying care to “wait and see” or to try remedies that are not appropriate. If the person cannot be woken, is not breathing normally, is blue, or appears to be in shock, emergency services must be contacted immediately.
