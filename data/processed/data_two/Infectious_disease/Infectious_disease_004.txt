Respiratory infections affect the nose, throat, airways, or lungs and include common colds, influenza, COVID-19, bronchitis, and pneumonia. They are often spread by droplets or contact with contaminated hands and surfaces. These infections are common across all ages, but infants, older adults, pregnant people, and people with chronic illness can be at higher risk for complications.

Symptoms vary depending on the infection and severity. Common symptoms include cough, sore throat, runny nose, fever, body aches, fatigue, and wheezing. Mild cases often improve with rest, fluids, and symptom relief, while more severe cases can involve trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, or persistent high fever. People may also have headache, loss of appetite, or worsening weakness.

Infections such as influenza and COVID-19 can spread rapidly in schools, workplaces, and crowded homes. Transmission often occurs when infected people cough, sneeze, speak, or touch shared objects. Good hygiene, ventilation, and avoiding close contact with people who are unwell help reduce spread. People with respiratory symptoms should try to cover coughs and sneezes, clean hands often, and stay home from work or school when they feel too unwell to participate or when public-health guidance recommends it.

Some respiratory infections affect the upper airways and cause mild discomfort, while others are more serious and may lower oxygen levels. Signs of more serious illness include fast breathing, shortness of breath, chest pain, blue or grey lips, confusion, severe weakness, or dehydration. Adults and caregivers should be alert for breathing difficulty, especially in infants and children. Children with severe cough, wheezing, or poor feeding need prompt medical attention.

Pregnant people are more susceptible to some respiratory infections and are also at higher risk of complications if they develop severe illness. Fever, breathing difficulty, chest pain, or worsening symptoms during pregnancy need timely assessment. Pregnant people should also take ordinary preventive measures seriously, including vaccination when appropriate and avoiding close contact with sick people when recommended.

Infants and young children can develop respiratory illness quickly. They may not be able to describe their symptoms clearly, so caregivers need to watch for signs such as fast breathing, grunting, pulling in the muscles between the ribs, poor feeding, sleepiness, or blue lips. A child who is struggling to breathe or is unusually drowsy should be assessed urgently.

Treatment depends on the cause of the infection and the person’s age, health status, and symptoms. Some infections are managed with supportive care, fluids, and rest, while others may need medical review and specific treatment. It is important not to use antibiotics for viral respiratory infections unless there is a clear medical reason, as misuse contributes to antibiotic resistance. People should not request antibiotics or other prescriptions without a proper assessment.

Healthcare assessment is appropriate if symptoms become more severe, last beyond what is expected, or are accompanied by breathing difficulty, dehydration, persistent fever, or worsening weakness. In severe cases, emergency care is needed. Prevention, early recognition, and prompt care are the most effective ways to limit complications from respiratory illness.
