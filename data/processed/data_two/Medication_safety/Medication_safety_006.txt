Medication errors and accidental ingestion are common safety problems that can happen at home, school, or in the community. A medication error may include taking the wrong dose, taking a medicine at the wrong time, mixing medicines incorrectly, giving the wrong medicine, or using an incorrect measuring device. Accidental ingestion occurs when a person, especially a child or infant, takes a medicine or household product that was not meant for them.

These incidents can happen for many reasons: confusion between medicines, missing labels, poor storage, sharing medicines, or misunderstanding instructions. They are more likely when adults are tired, rushed, or caring for several family members. In homes, small children may mistake tablets, capsules, or liquids for sweets, especially if medicines are left accessible.

The first indicator of a possible medication problem is a sudden change in how a person is behaving or feeling. Symptoms may include vomiting, drowsiness, confusion, unusual agitation, breathing difficulty, rash, or collapse. In children, a medicine may cause unexpected sleepiness or difficulty waking. In adults, accidental overdose may lead to severe dizziness, chest pain, or altered consciousness.

If a medication error is suspected, the safest step is to check the product name, dose, and time taken, then contact a pharmacist, poison centre, or local emergency service according to local guidance. Do not try to “fix it” by giving extra medicine or making the person vomit unless a healthcare professional has told you to do so. Some substances can cause more harm if induced vomiting is attempted. Do not give alcohol or other substances without professional instructions.

The risk is highest for children and infants because even a small dose of some medications can be serious. The home should be prepared for emergencies by keeping medicines locked away, using child-resistant packaging correctly, and ensuring the number for poison information or emergency services is easy to find. Safe storage also applies to cleaning products, cosmetics, and some supplements.

People should also be careful with multi-ingredient products that can contain more than one active medicine, such as cold or pain medicines. Taking two products with the same active ingredient can unintentionally double the dose. This is a common cause of accidental overdose. Checking labels and keeping a written list of current medicines helps reduce confusion.

Urgent emergency care is needed if a person has trouble breathing, severe drowsiness, seizures, persistent vomiting, collapse, chest pain, blue lips, or unresponsiveness. Poisoning from certain medicines, including some pain medicines and sedatives, can become life-threatening. In these situations, emergency services or the nearest urgent medical facility should be contacted immediately.

Preventing medication errors is not difficult but requires attention. Clear labelling, secure storage, careful dosing, and a willingness to ask questions when a medicine seems unclear all reduce risk. The safest response to any suspected overdose or ingestion is to seek professional help quickly.
